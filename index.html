Defining the spec. Verifying the run.

A bad batch rarely starts with a bad factory. It starts with a loose spec sheet.

In overseas food and beverage manufacturing, the gap between the sample you approved and the product that arrives usually traces back to the contract specification. Where a spec relies on vague language, both sides fill the gaps with their own assumptions, and you only find out when the first commercial run lands.

I created Mekong QA Advisory to close that gap, whether you are establishing specs before your first run or retrofitting a product specification, packaging specification, and verification protocol onto an existing co-packer contract when quality issues arise. I write the objective technical standards and verify compliance directly on the factory floor in Southeast Asia, so you keep leverage over your production and your capital.

The 3-stage production control pipeline

End-to-end quality assurance, or mid-stream crisis triage. Start at Stage 1 for a new product, or step in at Stage 2 or 3 to triage an active production issue or hold an out-of-spec batch. Strict financial and technical gates protect your capital at every phase.

Stage 1Line trial

Micro-run, about 500–1,000 units

  • Run on actual commercial factory machinery.
  • Air-freight samples to the founder and QA architect.
  • Adjust process specs; sign 3 Golden Samples.
Risk gate 1Commercial deposits and raw material purchases stay frozen until Golden Sample sign-off.

Stage 2Commercial scale

Mass batch, 20,000+ units

  • Full batch produced using locked Stage 1 parameters.
  • Finished pallets wrapped and held at the factory site.
Risk gate 2Container loading waits for inspection. Payment holds follow the terms in your agreement.

Stage 3Pre-shipment inspection

Floor sampling and release

  • Random sampling off warehouse pallets.
  • Checked against the product specification, packaging specification, and Golden Sample.
Risk gate 3Final payment and container sealing wait until you have reviewed the PSI report and approved release.

The Golden Sample signed at Stage 1 becomes the baseline for your product specification. Its ranges start as initial ranges through the first commercial lots in Stage 2, then tighten by a method agreed in advance. See how the documents fit together →
