Defining the spec. Verifying the run.

A bad batch rarely starts with a bad factory. It starts with a loose spec sheet.

In overseas food and beverage manufacturing, the gap between the sample you approved and the product that arrives usually traces back to the specification. Where a spec relies on vague language, both sides fill the gaps with their own assumptions, and you only find out when the first commercial run lands.

I created Mekong QA Advisory to close that gap, whether you are setting your specs before your first run or adding a product spec and verification protocol to an existing co-packer agreement after quality issues appear. I write the measurable standards, and I go to the factory floor in Southeast Asia and verify the run against them myself. The documents are yours, and they work at any factory, so you keep leverage over your production and your capital.

The 3-stage production control pipeline

End-to-end quality assurance, or mid-stream crisis triage. Start at Stage 1 for a new product, or step in at Stage 2 or 3 to triage an active production issue or hold an out-of-spec batch. Strict financial and technical gates protect your capital at every phase.

Stage 1Line trial

Micro-run, about 500–1,000 units

  • Run on actual commercial factory machinery.
  • Air-freight samples to the founder and QA architect.
  • Adjust process specs; sign 3 Golden Samples.
Risk gate 1Commercial deposits and raw material purchases stay frozen until Golden Sample sign-off.

Stage 2Commercial scale

Mass batch, 20,000+ units

  • Full batch produced using locked Stage 1 parameters.
  • Finished pallets wrapped and held at the factory site.
Risk gate 2Container loading waits for inspection. Payment holds follow the terms in your agreement.

Stage 3Pre-shipment inspection

Floor sampling and release

  • Random sampling off warehouse pallets.
  • Measured against the product spec, including pH, Brix, water activity, salinity, and ABV where applicable, and checked against the Golden Sample.
Risk gate 3A commercial quality gate: final payment and container sealing wait until you have reviewed the inspection report and approved release.

The Golden Sample signed at Stage 1 sets your technical baseline. Parameters operate within provisional tolerance bounds for the initial commercial production run, automatically locking into tight, non-negotiable ranges once baseline plant capability is established.
