Co-packing quality assurance architecture

Translating culinary character into rigid, enforceable contract annexes: Schedules A & B.

Specification Without Defined Tolerances Isn't A Quality Standard.

When a co-packing agreement relies on loose targets without upper and lower thresholds, the definition of an “acceptable batch” is left open to interpretation. If a production run strays from your approved sample, a vague spec sheet leaves you with unsellable inventory and no technical standing to demand a re-run. Schedules A and B convert your culinary benchmark into clear, measurable parameters that both sides agree to before capital is released.

A standard Master Services Agreement handles financial terms and intellectual property, but it consistently leaves open how consistency is tested on the plant floor, how samples are pulled, and what technical thresholds actually trigger a rejected batch. Where the specification is silent, both sides are left guessing.

I build the technical annexes to your contract, Schedules A and B, that translate your culinary identity into objective, data-driven targets. By bridging Red Seal culinary expertise with plant-floor engineering, we align the co-packer's operational incentives with yours before the first commercial run. If you're already in production and hitting quality friction, this same architecture retrofits your agreement with enforceable addendums to establish control without needing to move to a new plant.

Core deliverables

The technical specifications, quality standards, and operational guardrails your co-packing contract relies on.

  • Product specifications (Schedule A): Objective, non-negotiable parameters locking in your product's culinary identity and technical bounds. We define target values and hard tolerance limits for pH, Brix, viscosity, water activity (aw), fill weights, texture, piece-size distribution, color, and sensory profile.
  • Quality & testing protocols (Schedule B): Line-level requirements covering inline sampling frequencies, standard measurement methodologies, and the exact QA verification steps required during active production runs.
  • Data-based rejection criteria: Unambiguous, objective grounds for refusing out-of-spec production before non-conforming inventory ever leaves the facility dock.
  • Corrective action frameworks (CAPA): Standardized root-cause investigation and corrective action procedures when a batch strays off-spec, assigning clear operational accountability to the facility.
  • Acceptance & lot release: Objective technical criteria for lot sign-off, ensuring independent lab checks and verified Certificate of Analysis (COA) records match specification before financial milestones clear.
  • Built for legal integration: A clean, production-ready technical package designed for your legal counsel to incorporate directly into your master co-packing agreement as enforceable annexes.
