Co-Packer Quality Terminology & Specifications Glossary

This plain-language glossary explains the documents, contracts, measurements, and standards that matter when a food or beverage brand works with an overseas co-packer.

Start Here

Co-packer

A factory that makes and packs your product under your brand[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span). Also called a co-manufacturer or contract manufacturer[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span).

Golden sample

The approved finished product that everything else is measured against[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span). A lot passes when it matches the golden sample within the ranges in the product spec[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span).

See also: product spec[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span)

Spec drift

The slow change in a product from run to run, such as a little less sugar, a thinner texture, or a swapped ingredient[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span). Each change is small, but together they leave a product that no longer matches what you approved[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span). Written ranges are how you catch it[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span).

First-run verification

Checking the first commercial run against the spec before it ships, on the factory floor, with calibrated instruments[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span). It shows whether the spec works in practice[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span).

See also: verification protocol[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span)

Target and tolerance

The target is the number you are aiming for (say, Brix 62)[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span). The tolerance is the range around it that still counts as acceptable (say, 60 to 64)[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span). A reading outside the range fails[span_15](start_span)[span_15](end_span).

The Documents

Product spec

A written definition of what the finished product must be: measurable targets and ranges, defect classes, and a sensory description[span_16](start_span)[span_16](end_span). It belongs to the brand and works at any factory, so you can send the same spec to several factories and compare their quotes[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span). It describes the end product, not the factory’s equipment[span_18](start_span)[span_18](end_span).

See also: method card, verification protocol[span_19](start_span)[span_19](end_span)

Verification protocol

The companion document for checking[span_20](start_span)[span_20](end_span). It says how each lot is sampled, measured and accepted or rejected against the spec, so a team member or inspector can decide in minutes[span_21](start_span)[span_21](end_span).

See also: AQL, defect class[span_22](start_span)[span_22](end_span)

Method card

A one-page instruction for a single measurement, covering the instrument, preparation, temperature, number of readings, calibration and what to record[span_23](start_span)[span_23](end_span). A second person or a lab should be able to repeat it and get the same result[span_24](start_span)[span_24](end_span).

Quality agreement

The part of the factory contract that covers quality: who is responsible for what, how lots are rejected, and what happens next[span_25](start_span)[span_25](end_span). The product spec and verification protocol attach to it[span_26](start_span)[span_26](end_span). A lawyer drafts the contract itself[span_27](start_span)[span_27](end_span).

Inspection report

The written result of checking one lot against the spec[span_28](start_span)[span_28](end_span). It records the lot, the sampling used, every reading, the sensory result and the decision, and it is signed by the inspector[span_29](start_span)[span_29](end_span). It states the technical result only; the brand decides what happens to the lot commercially[span_30](start_span)[span_30](end_span).

See also: pre-shipment inspection[span_31](start_span)[span_31](end_span)

Certificate of Analysis (CoA)

The factory’s own paper listing the test results for a lot[span_32](start_span)[span_32](end_span). It is useful, but it is the factory’s claim[span_33](start_span)[span_33](end_span). Verification compares those numbers with independent readings and flags any difference before payment is released[span_34](start_span)[span_34](end_span).

See also: accredited lab[span_35](start_span)[span_35](end_span)

Retained reference

A sealed, labeled unit from the approved product, stored under set conditions and replaced on a schedule[span_36](start_span)[span_36](end_span). It is the physical answer to “what was this supposed to taste and look like?[span_37](start_span)”[span_37](end_span)

Contracts and Money

Co-packing agreement

The main contract between a brand and a factory[span_38](start_span)[span_38](end_span). It covers price, volumes, term, ownership and liability[span_39](start_span)[span_39](end_span). The product spec and verification protocol attach to it, usually through the quality agreement[span_40](start_span)[span_40](end_span). A lawyer drafts it, and your documents are written so that lawyer can embed them[span_41](start_span)[span_41](end_span).

Payment gate

A payment tied to a result instead of a date[span_42](start_span)[span_42](end_span). A common setup is a deposit up front and the balance only after a lot passes inspection[span_43](start_span)[span_43](end_span). This is where verification directly protects your money[span_44](start_span)[span_44](end_span).

Rejection right

The brand’s contractual right to refuse a lot that fails the spec, and what the factory must then do[span_45](start_span)[span_45](end_span). It only works if the spec is specific enough to prove the failure[span_46](start_span)[span_46](end_span).

Corrective action

The factory’s written response to a failed lot: what went wrong, what it will change, and by when[span_47](start_span)[span_47](end_span). Your protocol sets when one is required[span_48](start_span)[span_48](end_span).

Incoterms

Short standard codes for who pays for and carries the risk of a shipment at each stage[span_49](start_span)[span_49](end_span). Two you will hear often are EXW, where the buyer takes over at the factory door, and FOB, where the seller is responsible until the goods are loaded on the ship[span_50](start_span)[span_50](end_span). They decide who bears the loss on a rejected lot[span_51](start_span)[span_51](end_span).

Measurements

Brix (°Bx)

The share of dissolved solids in a liquid, mostly sugar, measured with a refractometer[span_52](start_span)[span_52](end_span). A reading of 62 °Bx means the product is about 62% dissolved solids by weight[span_53](start_span)[span_53](end_span). It is a quick check that a jam, sauce or syrup has not been thinned out or over-cooked[span_54](start_span)[span_54](end_span). Seasonal fruit varies, so the range needs room for that[span_55](start_span)[span_55](end_span).

See also: target and tolerance[span_56](start_span)[span_56](end_span)

pH

A scale from 0 to 14 showing how acidic something is[span_57](start_span)[span_57](end_span). Lower is more acidic[span_58](start_span)[span_58](end_span). It shapes taste and stability, so it is a core quality measure[span_59](start_span)[span_59](end_span). Any safety limit tied to pH is set by the plant and the brand’s regulatory advisor, not by the spec[span_60](start_span)[span_60](end_span).

See also: calibration[span_61](start_span)[span_61](end_span)

Water activity (aw)

A scale from 0 to 1 showing how much of the water in a product is free instead of locked up in the ingredients[span_62](start_span)[span_62](end_span). It affects texture, shelf life and how the product ages[span_63](start_span)[span_63](end_span). It is usually measured by a lab at first and later with an on-site meter[span_64](start_span)[span_64](end_span).

Consistency (Bostwick)

How thick or runny a product is, measured by how far it flows along a small trough in a set time[span_65](start_span)[span_65](end_span). A farther flow means a thinner product[span_66](start_span)[span_66](end_span). It replaces “a bit thicker, please” with a number a factory can hit[span_67](start_span)[span_67](end_span).

Salinity

The salt content of a product, usually as a percentage, measured with a meter or a lab method[span_68](start_span)[span_68](end_span). It matters for taste, and a small change is noticeable[span_69](start_span)[span_69](end_span).

Alcohol by volume (ABV)

The percentage of the liquid that is alcohol, relevant only to products that contain it, such as some sauces, marinades and beverages[span_70](start_span)[span_70](end_span).

Color (L*a*b*)

A way to give a color three numbers: L* for lightness, a* for green to red, and b* for blue to yellow[span_71](start_span)[span_71](end_span). Locking in numbers replaces “make it look like the sample,” which depends on lighting and on who is looking[span_72](start_span)[span_72](end_span). The lab sets the baseline from the approved product[span_73](start_span)[span_73](end_span).

See also: Delta E[span_74](start_span)[span_74](end_span)

Delta E (ΔE)

A single number showing how far apart two colors are[span_75](start_span)[span_75](end_span). As a rule of thumb, around 1 is barely noticeable to a trained eye and 2 to 3 is a common commercial tolerance[span_76](start_span)[span_76](end_span). The spec sets the limit for your product[span_77](start_span)[span_77](end_span).

Fill and net content

How much product is in the pack, not counting the container[span_78](start_span)[span_78](end_span). Net weight is the product alone, gross weight includes the packaging, and tare is the packaging’s own weight[span_79](start_span)[span_79](end_span). Checking fill catches under- and over-filling[span_80](start_span)[span_80](end_span).

Calibration

Checking an instrument against a known reference before you use it, so its readings can be trusted[span_81](start_span)[span_81](end_span). A reading from an uncalibrated meter proves little in a dispute[span_82](start_span)[span_82](end_span).

Baseline

The first measurements taken from the approved product, which become the starting point for the spec’s targets[span_83](start_span)[span_83](end_span). An accredited lab sets them, and your own instruments are checked against them[span_84](start_span)[span_84](end_span).

See also: golden sample[span_85](start_span)[span_85](end_span)

Accredited lab

A lab formally recognized as competent to run specific tests[span_86](start_span)[span_86](end_span). It matters because its result is the one that settles a dispute over a failed lot[span_87](start_span)[span_87](end_span). Check that it is accredited for the exact test you need[span_88](start_span)[span_88](end_span).

Sensory

Sensory analysis

Judging a product with the human senses (sight, smell, taste, touch) in a structured, repeatable way[span_89](start_span)[span_89](end_span). Done properly, it turns “tastes right” into something a second person can check[span_90](start_span)[span_90](end_span).

Flavor profile

A written description of how the approved product should taste and smell, in defined terms and with a score for each attribute[span_91](start_span)[span_91](end_span). It is what a second taster uses to judge a new lot against the golden sample[span_92](start_span)[span_92](end_span).

See also: reference vocabulary, scale and anchor[span_93](start_span)[span_93](end_span)

Taste, odor and flavor

Three different things[span_94](start_span)[span_94](end_span). Taste is what your tongue detects (sweet, sour, salty, bitter, umami)[span_95](start_span)[span_95](end_span). Odor is what you smell[span_96](start_span)[span_96](end_span). Flavor is the combined impression of taste, smell and sensations like heat or cooling while you eat[span_97](start_span)[span_97](end_span). Defining which one you mean avoids confusion[span_98](start_span)[span_98](end_span).

Aroma

In everyday English, a smell, often a pleasant one[span_99](start_span)[span_99](end_span). The standard notes that English and French use the word differently, so it is worth defining it in the spec when a team works in more than one language[span_100](start_span)[span_100](end_span).

Off-note

A smell or taste that does not belong in the product, usually a sign something has changed or gone wrong[span_101](start_span)[span_101](end_span). Your spec lists the off-notes that apply to the product, and a listed one has zero tolerance[span_102](start_span)[span_102](end_span).

See also: critical off-note[span_103](start_span)[span_103](end_span)

Critical off-note

An off-note serious enough that you stop and act, such as rancid, fermented, moldy, chemical or machine oil[span_104](start_span)[span_104](end_span). The rule is to report to the brand immediately and hold the lot[span_105](start_span)[span_105](end_span). A quality off-note, such as slightly flat or a little burnt, goes to a blind panel instead[span_106](start_span)[span_106](end_span).

Off-flavor vs. taint

Both are wrong tastes, but with different causes[span_107](start_span)[span_107](end_span). An off-flavor is an atypical taste that usually comes from the product itself changing or deteriorating[span_108](start_span)[span_108](end_span). A taint is a foreign taste or smell that comes from outside, such as machine oil or a cleaning agent[span_109](start_span)[span_109](end_span). Naming the cause helps decide what to ask the factory[span_110](start_span)[span_110](end_span).

Texture and mouthfeel

How a product feels in the mouth: thick, smooth, grainy, creamy, sticky, fizzy[span_111](start_span)[span_111](end_span). Consistency measurements cover the physical side, and the flavor profile covers what a taster notices[span_112](start_span)[span_112](end_span).

Blind comparison

Tasting a lot side by side with the golden sample without knowing which is which, usually with a second person who codes the samples[span_113](start_span)[span_113](end_span). It removes expectation, which is the most common source of sensory error[span_114](start_span)[span_114](end_span).

See also: bias[span_115](start_span)[span_115](end_span)

Bias

A distortion in a judgment that has nothing to do with the product[span_116](start_span)[span_116](end_span). Two common kinds are expectation bias (you taste what you expect) and the halo effect (one good quality makes everything seem better)[span_117](start_span)[span_117](end_span). Coding the samples and using a written profile reduce both[span_118](start_span)[span_118](end_span).

Scale and anchor

A simple scoring scale, such as 1 to 5, with each point tied to the reference sample, so a “3” means the same thing to every taster[span_119](start_span)[span_119](end_span). Without anchors, scores drift from person to person[span_120](start_span)[span_120](end_span).

Panel and assessor

The group of people who judge the product, and each individual taster[span_121](start_span)[span_121](end_span). In practice your panel may be two people, such as you and the plant’s QA person, but the same rules apply: blind, coded and written down[span_122](start_span)[span_122](end_span).

Reference vocabulary

A published list of defined sensory terms, such as ISO 5492 for general terms and category lists for specific products, used so “burnt” or “grassy” means the same thing to everyone[span_123](start_span)[span_123](end_span). Your spec matches the brand’s own words to these terms and cites the source[span_124](start_span)[span_124](end_span).

See also: flavor profile[span_125](start_span)[span_125](end_span)

Sampling and Inspection

Pre-shipment inspection

A check of a finished lot, on the factory floor, before it ships and before the balance is paid[span_126](start_span)[span_126](end_span). It confirms the lot matches the product spec, using calibrated instruments and a written verification protocol[span_127](start_span)[span_127](end_span). It is a quality check on what was ordered, and it does not cover the factory’s food safety system[span_128](start_span)[span_128](end_span).

See also: inspection report[span_129](start_span)[span_129](end_span)

Lot

One production run, or one defined portion of it, treated as a single unit for inspection[span_130](start_span)[span_130](end_span). The protocol says what counts as a lot, and the inspector confirms the size from the plant’s count and their own[span_131](start_span)[span_131](end_span).

Sample

The handful of units pulled from the lot and checked, standing in for the whole lot[span_132](start_span)[span_132](end_span). The inspector chooses the units, never the plant, because a sample the factory picked tells you only about the units it wanted you to see[span_133](start_span)[span_133](end_span).

Sampling plan

The rule that says how many units to pull from a lot of a given size and how many problems you can find before the lot fails[span_134](start_span)[span_134](end_span). Bigger lots get bigger samples[span_135](start_span)[span_135](end_span). The plan is fixed in the protocol before the visit, not decided on the day[span_136](start_span)[span_136](end_span).

AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit)

The level of defects you agree, in advance, to tolerate in regular production[span_137](start_span)[span_137](end_span). It is not a promise of zero defects[span_138](start_span)[span_138](end_span). It is the line the sampling plan is built around[span_139](start_span)[span_139](end_span). A stricter AQL means fewer defects are allowed and more units are pulled[span_140](start_span)[span_140](end_span).

See also: ISO 2859-1[span_141](start_span)[span_141](end_span)

Defect class (major and minor)

A label for how serious a failure is, agreed with the brand attribute by attribute before anything goes wrong[span_142](start_span)[span_142](end_span). A major defect matters to the buyer or consumer, such as a color far out of range or a short fill[span_143](start_span)[span_143](end_span). A minor defect is a smaller departure[span_144](start_span)[span_144](end_span). Each class gets its own AQL[span_145](start_span)[span_145](end_span).

Ac / Re (accept and reject numbers)

The two numbers from the sampling table for each defect class[span_146](start_span)[span_146](end_span). If the number of failing units is at or below Ac, the lot passes for that class[span_147](start_span)[span_147](end_span). If it reaches Re, the lot fails[span_148](start_span)[span_148](end_span). The inspector reads them from the protocol and does not change them on site[span_149](start_span)[span_149](end_span).

Inspection level

How much sampling a check calls for[span_150](start_span)[span_150](end_span). The usual starting point is a general level for checks that leave the unit intact[span_151](start_span)[span_151](end_span). A special, smaller level suits tests that destroy the unit or cost a lot, such as opening a sealed pack for a lab test, where a smaller sample is acceptable[span_152](start_span)[span_152](end_span).

Switching rules

The standard’s way of changing how hard you inspect based on recent results[span_153](start_span)[span_153](end_span). Lots that keep passing can move to lighter checking, and a run of failures moves you to tighter checking[span_154](start_span)[span_154](end_span). Someone keeps a lot-history record to track it[span_155](start_span)[span_155](end_span).

Non-destructive vs. destructive test

A non-destructive check, such as a visual check or a weight, leaves the unit sellable[span_156](start_span)[span_156](end_span). A destructive one, such as opening the pack to measure pH, uses it up[span_157](start_span)[span_157](end_span). That is why they use different sample sizes[span_158](start_span)[span_158](end_span).

Disposition

What happens to a lot after the result: accept, reject, hold, or rework with re-inspection[span_159](start_span)[span_159](end_span). The inspector reports the technical result, and the brand decides the disposition[span_160](start_span)[span_160](end_span).

Hold

Keeping a lot from shipping while a question is open, such as a listed off-note or a disputed reading[span_161](start_span)[span_161](end_span). The factory quarantines the lot under its own procedures, and the brand decides what happens next[span_162](start_span)[span_162](end_span).

Split sample

A sealed, labeled set of units from the same lot, kept apart for lab confirmation if the factory disputes a result[span_163](start_span)[span_163](end_span). The lab’s result decides the dispute[span_164](start_span)[span_164](end_span).

See also: chain of custody[span_165](start_span)[span_165](end_span)

Chain of custody

The paper trail showing who held a sample and when, from the moment it was drawn to the moment the lab opened it[span_166](start_span)[span_166](end_span). It is what lets a lab result stand up in a disagreement[span_167](start_span)[span_167](end_span).

Traceability (lot code)

The date or batch marking on each unit that links it back to its production run[span_168](start_span)[span_168](end_span). It lets you identify which units are affected if a lot has a problem[span_169](start_span)[span_169](end_span).

Standards

ISO standards

Documents published by the International Organization for Standardization that set agreed methods and vocabulary for things like sampling, testing and sensory work[span_170](start_span)[span_170](end_span). They are copyrighted and sold by ISO, so this glossary explains what each one is for and points you to the official text[span_171](start_span)[span_171](end_span). It does not reproduce them[span_172](start_span)[span_172](end_span).

ISO 2859-1 (sampling by attributes)

The standard behind your sampling plans[span_173](start_span)[span_173](end_span). It is the rulebook for how many units to pull from a lot, which AQL to use, and the accept and reject numbers that follow[span_174](start_span)[span_174](end_span). Your protocol is built on it, and every figure should be checked against a bought copy, never a free chart[span_175](start_span)[span_175](end_span).

See also: AQL, Ac / Re[span_176](start_span)[span_176](end_span)

ISO 5492 (sensory vocabulary)

The standard that defines sensory words, such as odor, flavor, off-flavor, taint and the names of tests[span_177](start_span)[span_177](end_span). Using it means “off-flavor” has one meaning for your team, the brand and the factory[span_178](start_span)[span_178](end_span).

See also: reference vocabulary[span_179](start_span)[span_179](end_span)

ISO 6658 (sensory methodology)

General guidance on how to run sensory work, such as how to set up tastings and handle samples[span_180](start_span)[span_180](end_span). It is the “how” that sits beside ISO 5492’s “what the words mean.[span_181](start_span)”[span_181](end_span)

ISO/IEC 17025 (laboratory competence)

The standard a lab is assessed against to be called accredited for a test[span_182](start_span)[span_182](end_span). It is why you ask for accreditation covering the exact test you need[span_183](start_span)[span_183](end_span). The lab’s result then carries weight in a dispute[span_184](start_span)[span_184](end_span).

See also: accredited lab[span_185](start_span)[span_185](end_span)

ISO 9001 (quality management)

A standard for how a company runs its quality system[span_186](start_span)[span_186](end_span). It can help you judge a factory before you hire it, but it says nothing about what your product must be, so it stays out of the product spec[span_187](start_span)[span_187](end_span).

Codex Alimentarius

A collection of international food standards and guidelines from the FAO and WHO[span_188](start_span)[span_188](end_span). For your work, it is a source for how a product category is commonly defined and what typical ranges look like[span_189](start_span)[span_189](end_span). It is free to read, but check the terms before quoting it[span_190](start_span)[span_190](end_span).

USDA Commercial Item Description (CID)

A short US government document, written for buying a type of food, such as hot sauce or pasta sauce[span_191](start_span)[span_191](end_span). Each one lays out types, characteristics, test methods and quality provisions for the category[span_192](start_span)[span_192](end_span). A CID is a general template, not a per-recipe spec, so it is useful as a cross-check on what a spec should cover[span_193](start_span)[span_193](end_span).

Standard of identity

A rule in some markets, including the US, that sets what a product must contain to carry a certain name, such as jam or preserves[span_194](start_span)[span_194](end_span). Which one applies depends on where the product is sold, and your spec leaves the legal question to the brand’s regulatory advisor[span_195](start_span)[span_195](end_span).

Standard test method (AOAC, ASTM)

A published, step-by-step procedure for a specific measurement, which a method card can cite by number[span_196](start_span)[span_196](end_span). These are copyrighted, so method cards describe the measurement in your own words and name the standard instead of copying it[span_197](start_span)[span_197](end_span).

Reference standards check

The first step in building a spec: searching for existing standards for the product category (Codex, ISO, a USDA CID, destination-market rules) before setting a target, and recording what you found with the date[span_198](start_span)[span_198](end_span). Anything useful becomes a candidate source for a baseline[span_199](start_span)[span_199](end_span).

Working with Factories

Face

A person’s standing and dignity in the eyes of others[span_200](start_span)[span_200](end_span). Saving face means avoiding embarrassment for yourself or for someone else, and giving face means showing respect[span_201](start_span)[span_201](end_span). It is why a factory may say “no problem” instead of “we cannot hit that range.[span_202](start_span)”[span_202](end_span) What this means for your spec: put ranges in writing and have the factory initial each critical parameter, so problems come up on paper before a run, not in front of an audience after it[span_203](start_span)[span_203](end_span).

Indirect communication

A “yes” can mean “I heard you” or “I will try,” not “I agree.[span_204](start_span)”[span_204](end_span) What this means for your spec: ask for the specific number or action instead of a yes, repeat it back in writing, and test it with a trial batch against the spec[span_205](start_span)[span_205](end_span).

Relationship before contract

Trust is often built through personal ties first, and a signed contract can be seen as the start of a relationship, with room to adjust, more than a fixed rulebook[span_206](start_span)[span_206](end_span). What this means for your spec: a neutral written spec turns “we will work it out” into a shared reference both sides can point to without blame[span_207](start_span)[span_207](end_span).

Hierarchy and the decision-maker

The senior person usually decides, and junior staff may be reluctant to contradict or reject[span_208](start_span)[span_208](end_span). What this means for your spec: agree up front who can approve a run and who can place a hold, and rely on an outside verifier whose findings do not depend on anyone’s rank[span_209](start_span)[span_209](end_span).
