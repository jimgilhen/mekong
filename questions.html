Questions to ask your co-packer before signing a contract

A co-packing contract covers price, volume, and ownership. These questions cover what it usually leaves out: how quality is checked and what happens when a lot fails. Ask them in writing and get the answers in writing.

The standard

  1. Will you sign our product spec and initial each critical range?Problems should come up on paper before a run, not in front of an audience after it.
  2. Who can approve a run and who can place a hold?The senior person usually decides. Agree up front who that is.

How quality is checked

  1. Who pulls the samples for inspection, your team or ours?A sample the factory picked only tells you about the units it wanted you to see.
  2. Can an independent inspector be on the floor during live production?Checks made after the line stops catch less than checks made while it runs.
  3. Will you send a certificate of analysis with every lot, and accept it being compared with independent readings?The certificate is the factory’s own claim. Independent readings show whether it holds.

When something goes wrong

  1. How is a disputed reading re-tested, by which accredited lab, and who pays?The lab’s result should settle the dispute. Decide the lab and the cost before the first lot.
  2. What happens to a failed lot, and by when must you submit a corrective action?Without a deadline, a failed lot turns into a negotiation.
  3. How long do you keep sealed retained samples of each lot?A retained sample is the physical answer to what the product was supposed to be.

Changes and money

  1. Can you change an ingredient, supplier, or process step without our written sign-off?Quiet changes are how a product drifts away from the one you approved.
  2. Is final payment tied to a passed pre-shipment inspection?A payment tied to a result protects you in a way a payment tied to a date can’t.
  3. Who carries the loss on a rejected lot once it leaves the dock?Incoterms such as EXW and FOB decide who bears the loss, so confirm the terms before you sign.

If a co-packer pushes back on the first question, that tells you how the first off-spec lot will go. Your counsel should word the answers into the agreement. This article is general information, not legal advice.
